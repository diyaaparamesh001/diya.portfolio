UX Case Study • 2026

A Missed
Connection

Aeroplane Mode made intentional disconnection effortless, but accidental activation could leave people unaware they were unreachable.

Aeroplane Mode is mainly designed to stop the phone’s wireless radios from communicating with cellular networks, including mobile networks, calls, SMS, and mobile data. But the real question is: are smartphone users aware of what happens when Aeroplane Mode is turned on?

The Premise

The Moment It
Clicked

A small issue. A big consequence.

The man accidentally turned on Aeroplane Mode. When he tried to make a call, he assumed his data had run out and didn’t realize it was affecting his connectivity. His family spent two days anxious and worried, with the situation escalating to the point where they nearly damaged the house and considered filing a police complaint.

A small system change can have a much bigger impact.

The interface showed that Aeroplane Mode was on, but not what that meant.

Design Focus

Designed for everyone, with older adults in mind.

Aeroplane Mode can be accidentally enabled by anyone. But this became especially concerning for older adults, who may be less familiar with subtle changes in smartphone interfaces.

What I Found

A familiar icon doesn't always mean a familiar function.

A personal incident made me question how well people understand Aeroplane Mode. I spoke to 21 people aged 40+ to see whether the confusion was limited to one experience.

JMIR mHealth and uHealth

Research done by JMIR mHealth and other various papers suggests that older users may not always understand conventional interface symbols without clear textual support.

67%

of participants aged above 40 had no idea what Aeroplane Mode does.

I surveyed 21 people aged 40+ and found that many weren't familiar with the feature or what it actually does.

What Users Reported

Key Insight

I realized the solution wasn't to interrupt users, but to make the consequences of Aeroplane Mode clearer at the right moment.

Defining the Intervention

Make the invisible consequence visible,
without interrupting the user's flow.

Make the change clear the moment it happens, so users immediately understand what Aeroplane Mode has affected. Keep the information brief and contextual, without interrupting their flow.

Why Nothing OS?

I chose Nothing OS because of its distinctive visual language and community driven approach made it an interesting environment to explore. I also wanted to work within an established design system, so I could focus on identifying the problem and designing a solution that felt native to the existing experience.

I referenced the latest Nothing OS 5.0 design system, announced August 25, 2026.

Directions Explored

Concept 1: Instant Full Screen Awareness

A full-screen message immediately tells users that Aeroplane Mode is on and cellular signal is unavailable.

  • Interrupts the user’s current activity and demands attention
  • Makes the change and its impact impossible to miss

Concept 2: Awareness at the Right Moment

The phone communicates the change when users attempt an affected action, like calling or using mobile data.

  • Contextual but limited awareness
  • Doesn’t address the initial confusion

Concept 3: A Reminder to Keep Users Aware

A reminder appears after some time, prompting users to notice that Airplane Mode is still on.

  • Simple but delayed awareness
  • Can be annoying and the timing matters

A subtle notification that lasts for 2 seconds informs the user that Aeroplane Mode is now on.

However, users may not understand what turning on Aeroplane Mode means for their connectivity.

Refined Concept

The same notification that says Aeroplane Mode is on, now showing that calls, mobile data, and SMS are unavailable.

A brief notification appears when Aeroplane Mode turns on, clearly explaining that calls, mobile data, and SMS are unavailable.

Why Lock Screen?

The lock screen acts as a second layer of protection, making sure the change is seen even if the notification panel is never opened.

System Efficiency

Less effort, more clarity.

Instead of leaving users to figure out why their connectivity has changed, the system provides the relevant context upfront so they can understand the change with less effort and fewer unnecessary steps.

  1. Immediate feedback — users know Aeroplane Mode has turned on without having to figure it out themselves.
  3. Less troubleshooting — they understand why calls, mobile data, and SMS aren't working.
  5. Fewer unnecessary steps — the system surfaces the information instead of making users dig through Settings
  7. Context at the right moment — the explanation appears when the state changes, when it's most relevant..
  9. Secondary confirmation — the lock screen notification gives users another chance to catch the change if they missed it

Testing

Initial Testing

20+ people tested the prototype across Quick Settings and the Lock screen. They were asked to explore the Quick Settings tiles and interact with the lock screen notification, then describe what they understood from the information presented.

21+

Participants

Among participants aged 40+, most understood Aeroplane Mode’s impact. Two participants said the notification at least made them aware it was on, which they otherwise wouldn’t know.

A moment of discovery:

“If this had popped up, I would have known why my calls weren’t going through.”

The Impact

Making an important system change
visible and understandable.

A subtle system change becomes a clear signal, helping users see what happened, understand its impact, and avoid confusion or missed connections. The information stays accessible without unnecessarily interrupting their flow.

A solution that went beyond the original problem

Designed to prevent confusion when Aeroplane Mode is accidentally turned on, the intervention also proved useful for people who forget to turn it off after a flight. 3 out of 21 frequent air travellers said they would find it useful, as they often forget to turn off Aeroplane Mode after flying.

Makes an important system change visible

Users know Aeroplane Mode has been turned on, instead of relying on a small status-bar icon.

Makes the consequence clear

They understand that calls, mobile data, and SMS are unavailable, reducing the need to figure out why connectivity stopped.

Prevents confusion and missed connections

For situations where calls unexpectedly fail, the information helps users recognize that Aeroplane Mode is the reason their connectivity is unavailable.

Conclusion

Turning a subtle change
into a clear signal

A study published in Global Environmental Change found that only 20% of the population has travelled by air, yet Aeroplane Mode is available on virtually every smartphone. Since a simple tap can significantly affect connectivity, making its consequences clear can help everyone understand what this system change means.
